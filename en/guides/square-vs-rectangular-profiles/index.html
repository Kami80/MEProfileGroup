Square vs Rectangular Hollow Sections: How to Choose

Square and rectangular hollow sections are both hollow steel profiles, but their geometry is different. Choose between them from the drawing, available space, loading direction and fabrication details—not from the assumption that one is always stronger or cheaper.

◷ 6 MIN READ•Reviewed: November 2026
TECHNICALMEPSELECT / SPECIFY / SOURCE
KEY TAKEAWAY

Section shape alone does not determine strength or final suitability.

01

The geometric difference

A square hollow section has equal outside width and height, such as 40 × 40 mm. A rectangular hollow section has different outside dimensions, such as 60 × 30 mm. Both may be available in different nominal wall thicknesses. The listed dimensions refer to the outside of the section; wall thickness is stated separately.

02

When to consider a square section

A square section may be appropriate when the drawing calls for equal outside dimensions. It can be considered for certain frames, supports, bases and fabricated assemblies. Suitability for a load-bearing or specialised application cannot be decided from shape alone: check the dimensions, thickness, material specification and project calculations.

03

When to consider a rectangular section

A rectangular section may suit a design with unequal outside dimensions or a specific space constraint. Its width-to-height ratio and orientation in the assembly matter. Keep the dimension order consistent in drawings and enquiries; for example, do not casually switch 60 × 30 to 30 × 60 without clarifying orientation.

04

Do not compare engineering performance by shape alone

Wall thickness, material properties, corner geometry, tolerances, length, support conditions, loading direction, connection details and service environment can all affect performance. Two sections with similar outside dimensions are not necessarily equivalent. For structural applications, use the calculations and standards specified by the project.

05

How to read size notation

The notation 40 × 40 × 2 generally describes a square section with 40 mm outside width, 40 mm outside height and 2 mm nominal wall thickness. The notation 60 × 30 × 2 describes a rectangular section with 60 mm and 30 mm outside dimensions and a 2 mm nominal wall. State the order and unit explicitly, then confirm the exact specification against the catalogue and supplier confirmation.

06

A practical selection checklist

1) Read the section shape and dimensions from the drawing. 2) Specify nominal wall thickness and length. 3) Check orientation and connection details. 4) State any required grade or standard. 5) Provide quantity and delivery destination. 6) For load-bearing use, obtain approval from the project engineer.

Frequently asked questions

Is a square section always stronger than a rectangular section?

No. Performance depends on dimensions, wall thickness, material properties, loading direction, connections and support conditions. Compare them using engineering calculations.

How should I state rectangular dimensions?

List outside width, outside height and nominal wall thickness in a consistent order and unit. Add orientation if it matters to the drawing.

Are all catalogue sizes always available?

The catalogue presents selected sizes. Availability and supply conditions must be confirmed for each enquiry.

How can I compare prices for the two shapes?

First match the product specification, thickness, standard, length, quantity and delivery terms. Then compare the quotations.
